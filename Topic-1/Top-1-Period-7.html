My teaching route
Lesson overview

Period 7 · Logical Binary Shifts

Cambridge focus: Topic 1.1 Number systems — logical binary shifts to the left and right. The textbook explains that a left shift is equivalent to multiplying by 2 and a right shift to dividing by 2, with empty positions replaced by zeroes. fileciteturn6file2L166-L178

Success criteria: Students can perform logical left and right shifts, explain the effect of each shift on a positive binary value, and recognise when repeated shifts exceed the useful range of an 8-bit register.

Prior learning: Period 6 — overflow and the limits of an 8-bit representation.

Key vocabulary: logical shift, shift left, shift right, bit, register, multiply, divide, power of 2, zero-fill, overflow.

Teacher preparation

Before students enter

  • Display an 8-bit binary number and its place values.
  • Prepare examples of one-place and multiple-place left/right shifts.
  • Keep the rule visible: left shift → ×2 per place; right shift → ÷2 per place.
  • Remind students that this lesson uses logical shifts: zeroes enter the empty positions.
  • Prepare one example where shifting left loses a bit in an 8-bit register.
Teaching principle: students should connect the visual movement of bits with the mathematical effect, rather than treating shifting as a mechanical string operation.
0–5 min · Retrieval

Reconnect with representation

  1. What is the denary value of 00000101₂?
  2. What is the effect of multiplying a number by 2?
  3. Why can an 8-bit register cause a value to be lost?
  4. What happens when a ninth bit is generated?
Bridge: A shift changes the positions of bits. Today we investigate exactly how that changes the value.
5–13 min · Teach

Logical shift left

Move every bit one position to the left. The empty position on the right is filled with 0.

00100101
↓ shift left 1
01001010

00100101₂ = 37₁₀ and 01001010₂ = 74₁₀.

One left shift = ×2

Two left shifts = ×4, three left shifts = ×8, and so on.

13–21 min · Teach

Logical shift right

Move every bit one position to the right. The empty position on the left is filled with 0.

00100100
↓ shift right 1
00010010

00100100₂ = 36₁₀ and 00010010₂ = 18₁₀.

One right shift = ÷2

Two right shifts = ÷4, three right shifts = ÷8.

21–29 min · Model

Multiple shifts

Model a three-place right shift:

01101000
→ 00110100  (1)
→ 00011010  (2)
→ 00001101  (3)

01101000₂ = 104₁₀. After three right shifts, the result is 13₁₀.

Check: 104 ÷ 2³ = 104 ÷ 8 = 13.

The textbook likewise describes each right shift as division by 2 and warns that excessive shifting can lose significant bits. fileciteturn6file2L166-L178

29–36 min · Guided practice

Think → Pair → Explain

Perform the requested shift and calculate the denary value before and after.

  1. 00101100₂ — shift left 1 place.
  2. 01010000₂ — shift right 2 places.
  3. 00010110₂ — shift left 2 places.
  4. 01111000₂ — shift right 3 places.
Teacher move: Ask students to predict the denary result before physically shifting the bits.
36–42 min · Differentiation

Three levels of challenge

Support: Use 4-bit values and perform one shift at a time.

Core: Use 8-bit values and perform two- or three-place shifts.

Challenge: Explain what happens when a left shift pushes a 1 beyond the left edge of an 8-bit register.

Key idea: shifting is only guaranteed to behave like ×2 or ÷2 while the required value remains representable in the available register.
42–47 min · Assessment

Mini-whiteboard check

  1. Shift 00010110₂ left by 1.
  2. Shift 01010000₂ right by 2.
  3. What mathematical operation does a left shift by 3 places represent?
Look for: students who shift the bits but fail to insert zeroes, or who reverse the multiplication/division relationship.
47–50 min · Exit ticket

Three questions before you leave

  1. Perform 00111000₂ → left shift 1.
  2. Perform 00111000₂ → right shift 2.
  3. State the mathematical effect of a right shift by 3 places.
Teacher decision: If students can perform and explain shifts, Period 8 can examine shift limits and then move into two's complement. If not, begin with a short shift drill.

Today you will learn how moving binary digits left or right changes the value.

Left → ×2 per place   |   Right → ÷2 per place

🎯 Learning goals

  • Perform logical left shifts.
  • Perform logical right shifts.
  • Insert zeroes into the empty positions.
  • Explain the multiplication/division effect.
  • Recognise when repeated shifts can exceed an 8-bit register's useful range.

1 · Logical shift left

Move every bit one position left and put 0 into the right-most position.

00100101
→ 01001010

37 becomes 74.

Left shift 1 = ×2

2 · Logical shift right

Move every bit one position right and put 0 into the left-most position.

00100100
→ 00010010

36 becomes 18.

Right shift 1 = ÷2

3 · Multiple shifts

Example: shift 01101000₂ right by three places.

01101000
00110100
00011010
00001101

104 ÷ 2³ = 13.

Three right shifts = divide by 8.

4 · Quick check

Q1: What does one left shift do?

Q2: What is 00010100₂ shifted right by 1?

5 · Practice — shift left

  1. 00101100₂ → left 1
  2. 00010110₂ → left 2
  3. 00110001₂ → left 1
  4. 00001001₂ → left 3
Answers:
1. 01011000
2. 01011000
3. 01100010
4. 01001000

6 · Practice — shift right

  1. 01010000₂ → right 1
  2. 01111000₂ → right 2
  3. 00110100₂ → right 2
  4. 11110000₂ → right 3
Answers:
1. 00101000
2. 00011110
3. 00001101
4. 00011110

7 · Connect to denary

Use the mathematical shortcut to predict the answer.

  • Left 1 → ×2
  • Left 2 → ×4
  • Left 3 → ×8
  • Right 1 → ÷2
  • Right 2 → ÷4
  • Right 3 → ÷8
Example: 48 shifted left 2 places = 48 × 4 = 192.

8 · When can things go wrong?

In a fixed 8-bit register, there is only room for eight bits. A left shift can push a bit beyond the left edge.

Important: The ×2 relationship works only while the result can be represented correctly in the available register.

Repeated shifts can eventually lose significant bits. The textbook illustrates this limitation for an 8-bit register. fileciteturn6file2L171-L178

9 · Exam practice

Question: Shift 00110100₂ three places to the right. Give the resulting binary number and its denary value.

00110100 → 00011010 → 00001101 → 00000110.
Result = 00000110₂ = 6₁₀.
Check: 52 ÷ 8 = 6.5, so the logical shift gives the integer result 6 because the fractional part is discarded.

10 · Common mistakes

  • Moving the bits in the wrong direction.
  • Forgetting to insert a zero into the empty position.
  • Confusing left shift with division.
  • Confusing right shift with multiplication.
  • Ignoring the fixed number of bits available in a register.
