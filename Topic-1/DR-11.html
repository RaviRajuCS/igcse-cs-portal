Sound Calculations
Sampling, Bit Depth & File Size

In DR-10 you learned how analogue sound becomes digital data. Now we will calculate how much data is produced. You will learn how sample rate, sample resolution, duration and number of channels affect the amount of storage required.

01

What Determines the Size of a Sound File?

For an uncompressed digital sound recording, four pieces of information are especially important.

1

Sample Rate

How many samples are taken every second?

44,100 samples/s

Higher sample rate → more samples → more data.

2

Sample Resolution

How many bits are used to represent each sample?

16 bits/sample

Higher bit depth → more bits per sample → more data.

3

Duration

How long is the recording?

60 seconds

Longer recording → more samples → more data.

4

Channels

How many separate audio channels are recorded?

Mono = 1
Stereo = 2

More channels → more sample data.

02

The Sound File-Size Formula

File size in bits = sample rate × sample resolution × duration × channels
Sample rate samples per second
Sample resolution bits per sample
Duration seconds
Channels 1, 2, etc.

Why does the formula work?

samples/s × bits/sample × seconds
=
bits

The word “samples” cancels with “per sample”, while seconds cancels with “per second”. This leaves the amount of data in bits.

Then Convert the Result

MiB

Use the unit requested by the question. Do not stop at bits if the question asks for bytes or MiB.

03

Important Storage Units

Unit Relationship Remember

04

Worked Examples

Example 1 — Mono Recording

A mono recording has a sample rate of 8,000 samples per second, a sample resolution of 8 bits, and lasts 10 seconds. Calculate the file size in bytes.

Step 1: Calculate bits
8,000 × 8 × 10 × 1 = 640,000 bits
Step 2: Convert bits to bytes
640,000 ÷ 8 = 80,000 bytes
Answer: 80,000 bytes
Example 2 — Stereo Recording

A stereo recording uses a sample rate of 44,100 samples/s, a sample resolution of 16 bits, and lasts 60 seconds. Calculate the file size in MiB.

Step 1: Calculate bits
44,100 × 16 × 60 × 2 = 84,672,000 bits
Step 2: Convert to bytes
84,672,000 ÷ 8 = 10,584,000 bytes
Step 3: Convert to KiB
10,584,000 ÷ 1024 = 10,335.9375 KiB
Step 4: Convert to MiB
10,335.9375 ÷ 1024 ≈ 10.09 MiB
Answer: ≈ 10.09 MiB
Example 3 — High-Quality Recording

A 24-bit stereo recording has a sample rate of 48,000 samples/s and lasts 120 seconds. Calculate the size in MiB.

Bits
48,000 × 24 × 120 × 2 = 276,480,000 bits
Bytes
276,480,000 ÷ 8 = 34,560,000 bytes
KiB
34,560,000 ÷ 1024 = 33,750 KiB
MiB
33,750 ÷ 1024 ≈ 32.96 MiB
Answer: ≈ 32.96 MiB
05

Mono and Stereo

Mono

●

One audio channel.

Channels = 1

Stereo

● ●

Two separate audio channels, normally left and right.

Channels = 2
Important:

If all other settings remain the same, a stereo recording requires twice as much raw sample data as a mono recording of the same duration.

06

How Each Factor Changes File Size

Change Effect on data Reason
Double sample rate Double Twice as many samples each second
Double bit depth Double Twice as many bits per sample
Double duration Double Twice as many seconds of recording
Mono → Stereo Double Two channels instead of one

Mistake 1

44,100 × 16 × 60 = 42,336,000 bits

The question says the recording is stereo.

Mistake 2

8,000 bits ÷ 1024 = 7.81 KiB

The student is trying to convert bits directly into KiB.

Mistake 3

1 MiB = 1,000 KiB

Mistake 4

Higher bit depth means fewer bits are stored.

The Reliable Exam Method

  1. Write the formula first. Do not immediately start multiplying numbers.
  2. Identify every factor. Sample rate, bit depth, duration and channels.
  3. Check units. Duration must be in seconds.
  4. Calculate the raw result in bits.
  5. Convert bits to bytes. Divide by 8.
  6. Continue converting if necessary. Divide by 1024 for each binary storage unit.
  7. State the unit clearly. A number without a unit may not receive full credit.
Golden rule:

Never divide by 1024 before converting bits to bytes.

The safe chain is:

bits → bytes → KiB → MiB → GiB

Challenge Problem

A school records a 3-minute stereo interview using a sample rate of 44,100 samples/s and a sample resolution of 16 bits.

Calculate the raw data size in MiB.

Step 1 — Convert minutes to seconds

3 × 60 = 180 seconds

Step 2 — Calculate bits

44,100 × 16 × 180 × 2 = 254,016,000 bits

Step 3 — Bytes

254,016,000 ÷ 8 = 31,752,000 bytes

Step 4 — KiB

31,752,000 ÷ 1024 ≈ 30,?

Step 5 — MiB

≈ 30.28 MiB

Final answer: approximately 30.28 MiB

Targeted Remediation

Before retaking the assessment, revisit these ideas:

  • File size = sample rate × bit depth × duration × channels.
  • The formula initially gives bits.
  • Divide by 8 to obtain bytes.
  • Divide by 1024 to move from bytes → KiB → MiB.
  • Mono = 1 channel; stereo = 2 channels.
  • Duration must be in seconds.
  • Increasing any multiplicative factor increases raw data size.
