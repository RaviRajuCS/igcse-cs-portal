Lesson overview

Period 2 · Converting Between Binary and Denary

Cambridge focus: Topic 1.1 Number systems — converting between positive denary and positive binary. The syllabus requires conversions in both directions, with integer values only and binary numbers up to 16 bits. fileciteturn5file0L11-L24

Success criteria: Students can convert positive binary integers to denary using place values and convert positive denary integers to binary using an appropriate method, showing clear working.

Prior learning: Period 1 — binary, denary, bases and binary place values.

Key vocabulary: denary, binary, base, bit, place value, positive integer, convert, most significant bit, least significant bit.

Teacher preparation

Before students enter

  • Open the webpage in full-screen mode.
  • Review the 8-bit place-value sequence: 128, 64, 32, 16, 8, 4, 2, 1.
  • Prepare mini-whiteboards or paper for rapid conversion questions.
  • Remind students that this period focuses on positive integers; two's complement is taught later.
  • Keep the distinction clear between a number's value and the binary representation used to store it.
0–5 min · Retrieval

Start with yesterday's learning

  1. What is the base of binary?
  2. What are the only two binary digits?
  3. Write the 8-bit place values from left to right.
  4. What does the right-most place represent?
Teacher move: Do not reteach the whole previous period. Use these questions to identify gaps before introducing conversion.
5–15 min · Teach

Binary → Denary

Model the method slowly. Place the binary digits above their powers of two, select the place values beneath every 1, then add them.

128
64
32
16
8
4
2
1

Model: 10110101₂

128 + 32 + 16 + 4 + 1 = 181

Therefore 10110101₂ = 181₁₀.

Check: Ask students to point to every 1 before doing the addition. This reduces place-value errors.
15–25 min · Teach

Denary → Binary

Use the largest suitable power of two and work from left to right. Put a 1 when the place value can be subtracted from the remaining value; otherwise put 0.

Model 1: Convert 45₁₀ to 8-bit binary.

32 fits → 1, remainder 13
16 does not fit → 0
8 fits → 1, remainder 5
4 fits → 1, remainder 1
2 does not fit → 0
1 fits → 1

Answer: 45₁₀ = 00101101₂

Teacher emphasis: Leading zeroes are included when the question asks for an 8-bit answer.

25–34 min · Guided practice

Think → Pair → Explain

Binary to denary:

  1. 00001111₂
  2. 00101010₂
  3. 11000001₂

Denary to 8-bit binary:

  1. 18₁₀
  2. 72₁₀
  3. 200₁₀

Require students to explain the place values they selected rather than giving only an answer.

34–42 min · Differentiation

Three levels of challenge

Support: use an 8-bit place-value table and convert 7, 12, 25 and 31.

Core: convert 53, 94, 127 and 173 into 8-bit binary.

Challenge: convert 101101011001₂ to denary, then convert 2749₁₀ to binary. The syllabus permits binary values up to 16 bits. fileciteturn5file0L36-L39

42–47 min · Assessment

Mini-whiteboard check

  1. Convert 01100101₂ to denary.
  2. Convert 156₁₀ to 8-bit binary.
  3. Explain why 00010110₂ and 10110₂ represent the same positive value.
Look for: students who know the procedure but repeatedly misalign the place values. This is a representation error, not necessarily a calculation error.
47–50 min · Exit ticket

Three questions before you leave

  1. Convert 00111001₂ to denary.
  2. Convert 99₁₀ to 8-bit binary.
  3. Write one sentence describing your method for denary → binary.
Teacher decision: If students can perform both directions accurately, Period 3 can introduce hexadecimal and binary/hexadecimal conversion. If not, begin Period 3 with a short targeted conversion recap.

🧭 Your learning route

1. Recall2. Binary → Denary 3. Denary → Binary4. Practice 5. Challenge6. Exit ticket

Today you will learn to move between two number systems. Always write your working — the method is as important as the final answer.

🎯 Learning goals

  • Convert a positive binary integer to denary.
  • Convert a positive denary integer to binary.
  • Use binary place values accurately.
  • Give an 8-bit answer when 8 bits are requested.
  • Explain the method clearly enough for another student to follow.

1 · Binary → Denary

Use the place values:

128
64
32
16
8
4
2
1

Example: 01011010₂

The 1s are under 64, 16, 8 and 2.

64 + 16 + 8 + 2 = 90
So 01011010₂ = 90₁₀.

Rule: add only the place values whose binary digit is 1.

2 · Denary → Binary

To convert a positive denary integer into binary, compare it with the place values from the largest to the smallest.

Example: 45₁₀

128
64
32
16
8
4
2
1

45 is less than 64 → 0
45 contains 32 → 1, remainder 13
13 is less than 16 → 0
13 contains 8 → 1, remainder 5
5 contains 4 → 1, remainder 1
1 is less than 2 → 0
1 contains 1 → 1

45₁₀ = 00101101₂

3 · Why leading zeroes matter

101101₂ and 00101101₂ have the same positive value: 45.

The second form explicitly uses 8 bits.

Exam tip: If the question says “give your answer as an 8-bit binary number”, include leading zeroes.

4 · Quick check

Q1: What is 00001111₂ in denary?

Q2: What is 18₁₀ as an 8-bit binary number?

5 · Practice — no peeking!

Convert binary to denary:

  1. 00001011₂
  2. 00110110₂
  3. 10010101₂

Convert denary to 8-bit binary:

  1. 27₁₀
  2. 64₁₀
  3. 149₁₀
Answers:
1. 11   2. 54   3. 149
4. 00011011   5. 01000000   6. 10010101

6 · Challenge

Try these without an 8-bit table:

  1. Convert 111100001111₂ to denary.
  2. Convert 2749₁₀ to binary.
  3. Explain how you know that 00101101₂ represents 45₁₀.
Answers:
1. 3855
2. 101010111101₂
3. 32 + 8 + 4 + 1 = 45.

7 · Common mistakes

  • Starting the place-value row with 1 instead of the largest required power of two.
  • Adding place values under 0s.
  • Forgetting that 1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 32... are powers of two.
  • Writing the correct value but not giving the requested 8-bit representation.
  • Confusing the conversion direction and using the wrong method.

8 · Exam practice

Question: Convert 01101011₂ into denary. Show your working.

01101011₂ = 64 + 32 + 8 + 2 + 1 = 107₁₀.

9 · Self-assessment

How confident are you now?
