Lesson overview

Period 4 · Serial & Parallel Transmission

Topic 2 · Data Transmission: Students learn the two main methods of sending multiple bits between devices: serial and parallel data transmission.

Success criteria: Students can define serial and parallel transmission, describe how bits travel in each method, compare their advantages and disadvantages, and select a suitable method for a given situation.

Prior learning: Period 3 — simplex, half-duplex and full-duplex describe the direction of communication. Today we study how bits are physically transmitted.

Key vocabulary: serial, parallel, bit, byte, channel, wire, synchronised, skewed, transmission rate, long distance, short distance.

My teaching route

Separate the two ideas

  1. Write ONE BIT on the board and introduce serial transmission.
  2. Show several wires carrying several bits at the same time for parallel transmission.
  3. Use a road analogy: one lane versus several lanes.
  4. Explain why serial works well over long distances.
  5. Explain why parallel is faster but can suffer from skew over longer distances.
  6. Finish by combining direction and method: serial can also be simplex, half-duplex or full-duplex.
Memory rule: Serial = one bit at a time · Parallel = several bits at the same time.

Serial data transmission

S

R

Serial data transmission occurs when data is sent one bit at a time over a single wire/channel. The bits are sent one after another as a single stream.

Advantages: works well over long distances and data arrives synchronised because one channel is used.
Disadvantage: it is slower than parallel transmission.

Textbook example: connecting a computer to a printer using a USB connection.

Parallel data transmission occurs when several bits of data, usually one byte, are sent down several channels/wires at the same time. Each channel carries one bit.

Advantage: faster than serial transmission.
Disadvantage: over longer distances the bits can become skewed and arrive unsynchronised or out of order.

Textbook context: internal computer circuits use parallel transmission because distances between components are short and high speed is important.

6 · What is skew?

In parallel transmission, bits travel along different wires. Over longer distances, they may arrive at different times.

Skewed data = bits arrive at the destination no longer synchronised.

7 · Exam practice

Question: Explain why serial transmission can be more suitable than parallel transmission over long distances.

Serial transmission uses a single channel and sends one bit at a time, so the data arrives synchronised. Parallel transmission uses several channels and, over longer distances, the bits can become skewed and arrive unsynchronised.

8 · Challenge

A computer's internal circuits need very high-speed transmission and the distance between components is very short. Which method is suitable?

9 · Common mistakes

  • Thinking serial means one direction only.
  • Thinking parallel automatically means full-duplex.
  • Forgetting that serial and parallel describe how bits are sent.
  • Forgetting that parallel transmission can suffer from skew over longer distances.
