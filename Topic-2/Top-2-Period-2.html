Lesson overview

Period 2 · Packet Switching & Routers

Topic 2 · Data Transmission: Students now move from the structure of a packet to the way packets travel across a network using packet switching.

Success criteria: Students can explain packet switching, describe how routers forward packets, explain why packets can take different routes, and explain how the destination reassembles the message.

Prior learning: Period 1 — data packets, headers, payloads, trailers and sequence numbers.

Key vocabulary: packet switching, router, routing, route, destination, forwarding, network congestion, reassembly.

My teaching route

Build the idea from the previous lesson

  1. Remind students that a message is divided into packets.
  2. Place four packets on the board.
  3. Draw two or three possible network routes.
  4. Show that packets do not necessarily have to follow the same route.
  5. Introduce the router as the device that forwards packets towards their destination.
  6. Finish by showing reassembly at the destination.
Core idea: packet switching allows packets to be forwarded independently through a network and reassembled at the destination.

5–13 min · Teach

What is packet switching?

Packet switching is a method of transmitting data in which a message is split into packets and the packets are sent across a network towards the destination.

Packets can be forwarded through different parts of the network depending on the available route.

Think: one message → many packets → network → destination → one reconstructed message.
13–22 min · Routers

What does a router do?

A router receives packets and decides where to forward them next so that they can move towards their destination.

  • Receives a packet.
  • Reads relevant addressing information.
  • Chooses a suitable next route.
  • Forwards the packet.
Important: a router is not the destination. It is an intermediate device that forwards traffic.

30–36 min · Why different routes?

Traffic and alternative paths

A network may have more than one possible route between two devices. If one route is busy or unavailable, packets may be forwarded through another route.

Key idea: packet switching can make efficient use of available network paths.

5 · Why different routes?

Networks can have several possible paths. A packet may be forwarded along a different path if another route is busy or unavailable.

Different routes do not mean different messages. The packets can still belong to the same original message.

7 · Your turn

Define packet switching.

A method of transmitting data in which a message is split into packets that are sent across a network towards the destination.

Explain why packets may take different routes.

A network can have several possible paths, and packets may be forwarded along different paths depending on the available route or network traffic.

8 · Exam practice

Question: Explain how packets are reassembled at the destination.

Sequence numbers identify the position of each packet. The receiving system uses these numbers to put packets into the correct order and reconstruct the original message.

9 · Common mistakes

  • Thinking every packet must follow the same route.
  • Thinking a router is the final destination.
  • Forgetting the role of sequence numbers.
  • Thinking packets are automatically received in the correct order.
