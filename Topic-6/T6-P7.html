Roles, Applications, Advantages and Disadvantages of Robots

This period moves from what a robot is to why robots are used. Students evaluate robot use in real-world scenarios and explain benefits and limitations.

Exam focus: Always connect an advantage or disadvantage to the particular robotic application being described.

3 · Advantages of robots

  • Safety: robots can perform dangerous tasks without putting a human directly in the hazardous environment.
  • Consistency: programmed movements can be repeated in the same way.
  • Precision: robots can carry out controlled movements for suitable tasks.
  • Speed: repetitive operations can be performed rapidly.
  • Continuous operation: robots can work for long periods without human fatigue.
  • Productivity: automation can increase the amount of work completed.
18–27 min · Teach

4 · Disadvantages of robots

  • Cost: robots can be expensive to purchase, install and maintain.
  • Job displacement: some tasks previously performed by people may become automated.
  • Maintenance: mechanical and electrical components can fail.
  • Programming dependence: an incorrect program can produce incorrect behaviour.
  • Limited flexibility: a robot may struggle with unexpected situations if it has not been programmed for them.
  • Safety risk from failure: a malfunctioning robot can cause injury or damage.
27–35 min · Worked examples

5 · Application analysis

Manufacturing: a robot performs repetitive welding. Benefit: consistent welding and reduced exposure to hazardous work. Drawback: purchase and maintenance costs can be high.
Exploration: a robot enters a dangerous environment to collect measurements. Benefit: reduces human exposure to danger. Drawback: communication, power or component failure may prevent the mission.
Domestic cleaning: a robot vacuum cleans floors automatically. Benefit: reduces human effort. Drawback: it may need maintenance and may not handle unusual obstacles effectively.
35–42 min · Evaluation

6 · Balanced judgement

A good evaluation does not simply say “robots are good” or “robots are bad”. It considers the purpose of the robot.

Example: For a robot working in a radioactive environment, safety may be a much more important advantage than speed. For a factory producing thousands of identical products, consistency and productivity may be especially important.
42–47 min · Guided workout

7 · Scenario evaluation

A hospital uses a robot to transport supplies between departments. Give two advantages and two disadvantages, explaining each in context.
Advantages: it can transport supplies repeatedly, reducing routine human workload; it can operate consistently according to its programmed route. Disadvantages: purchase and maintenance can be expensive; a fault could prevent supplies reaching the required department.
47–50 min · Exit ticket

8 · The evaluation formula

Point → because → consequence in this scenario.

Example: “A robot can work continuously because it does not become physically tired, so it can repeatedly transport supplies for long periods.”

8 · Scenario challenge — Medicine

A robot assists with a medical procedure requiring controlled, precise movements. Give one advantage and one possible disadvantage.
Advantage: controlled programmed movement can provide high consistency and precision for suitable tasks. Disadvantage: a malfunction or incorrect programming could have serious consequences and requires careful maintenance and supervision.

9 · Exam-style question

Question: A company is considering robots for repetitive factory work. Explain two advantages and two disadvantages.

Advantages: robots can repeat tasks quickly and consistently, increasing production; they can reduce the need for people to perform repetitive or hazardous work. Disadvantages: robots can be expensive to buy and maintain; faults or incorrect programming can stop production or cause incorrect actions.

10 · Evaluation challenge

Would a robot necessarily be the best choice for every task? Explain why or why not.
No. A robot is most suitable when its strengths match the task. A task requiring flexible human judgement, unexpected decisions or low-volume work may not justify the cost or programming needed for a robot.

11 · Common mistakes

  • Assuming robots are always faster or more accurate.
  • Ignoring cost and maintenance.
  • Giving advantages without linking them to the application.
  • Claiming a robot cannot fail.
  • Forgetting that incorrect programming can cause incorrect behaviour.

12 · Self-assessment

Can you explain why a robot is suitable for a task and evaluate both its benefits and limitations?
