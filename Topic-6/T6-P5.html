Topic 6 · Period 5

Introduction to Robotics

This period introduces robotics as an application of automated control. Students learn the basic parts of a robot and how sensing, processing and movement work together.

Cambridge 0984 focus: understand the characteristics and uses of robots, including their ability to sense their environment, process information and carry out programmed actions.

1 · What is a robot?

A robot is a programmable machine that can carry out tasks automatically or semi-autonomously. A robot normally combines:

SENSORS
→
CONTROL / PROCESSING
→
ACTUATORS

The robot can repeatedly sense its environment, process the information and produce an action.

0–8 min · Retrieval

2 · Robot components

ComponentPurpose
SensorDetects information about the robot's environment.
Processor / controllerProcesses sensor information and follows the programmed instructions.
ActuatorProduces movement or another physical action.
Power supplyProvides electrical energy to operate the robot.
ProgramDefines the instructions and behaviour of the robot.
8–18 min · Teach

3 · Robots can sense their environment

Robots may use sensors to detect conditions such as:

  • distance from an object
  • light level
  • temperature
  • touch/contact
  • position or movement
Example: A warehouse robot uses a distance sensor to detect an obstacle in its path. The controller processes the reading and changes the robot's movement.
18–27 min · Teach

4 · Robots need actuators to act

ActuatorPossible robot action
MotorDrive wheels, rotate an arm or move a joint.
Gripper mechanismPick up or release an object.
Servo motorMove a component to a controlled position.
Speaker/buzzerProduce an audible signal.
A robot's sensors provide information; its actuators allow it to do something physically.
27–35 min · Worked example

5 · Obstacle-avoiding robot

Distance Sensor
→
Controller
→
Motors
  1. The sensor measures the distance to an obstacle.
  2. The controller receives and processes the measurement.
  3. The program checks whether the obstacle is too close.
  4. If it is too close, the controller changes the motor commands.
  5. The robot stops, turns or changes direction.
35–42 min · Guided teaching

7 · Explain the robot

A warehouse robot must move around a building while avoiding obstacles. Explain how its sensor, controller and actuators work together.
A distance/proximity sensor detects obstacles. The controller processes the sensor data and follows the programmed instructions. It sends signals to motors, which move the robot or change its direction to avoid the obstacle.
47–50 min · Exit ticket

8 · Robot thinking

Remember: A robot does not “think” like a human. It uses sensors to obtain data and a programmed controller to determine the actions its actuators should perform.

9 · Exam-style question

Question: Explain how an obstacle-avoiding robot can detect an obstacle and change its direction.

A distance/proximity sensor detects the obstacle and sends data to the controller. The controller processes the data and checks the programmed condition. If the obstacle is too close, it sends signals to the motors/actuators to stop, turn or change direction.

10 · Robot vs ordinary automated system

Automated systemRobot
May control a fixed process.Often combines sensing, processing and physical movement.
Can use sensors and actuators.Uses sensors and actuators to interact with its environment.
May remain in one location.May be mobile or have moving joints.

11 · Common mistakes

  • Calling a motor a sensor.
  • Saying the robot “thinks” without explaining the programmed processing.
  • Forgetting that sensors provide input data.
  • Forgetting that actuators produce physical actions.
  • Listing robot applications without explaining why robots are suitable.
