Choosing the Right Robot for the Task

This period consolidates robotics by asking students to decide why a robot is suitable for a particular task and to justify the decision using characteristics, applications, advantages and limitations.

Exam skill: Do not say simply “robots are useful”. Explain how the robot's features match the task.

3 · Suitability depends on the task

  • Repetition: robots are useful when the same actions must be performed many times.
  • Danger: robots can work in environments where human exposure would be risky.
  • Precision: programmed movement can be useful for controlled tasks.
  • Continuous work: robots can operate for long periods without human fatigue.
  • Predictability: robots work well when the task can be described by programmed rules.
A task involving frequent unexpected decisions may be less suitable for a conventional programmed robot.
18–27 min · Teach

4 · Limitations to consider

LimitationWhy it matters
CostPurchase, installation and maintenance may be expensive.
ProgrammingUnexpected situations may not be handled if they were not considered in the program.
FailureSensor, controller, actuator or mechanical faults can stop the task.
SafetyA malfunctioning robot can create hazards.
FlexibilityA robot may be less adaptable than a person in an unfamiliar situation.
27–35 min · Worked example

5 · Hazardous exploration robot

Scenario: A robot is used to inspect an unsafe industrial area.
  1. Suitable: it can enter the hazardous area instead of sending a person.
  2. Sensing: sensors can collect information about the environment.
  3. Control: the controller processes readings and follows programmed instructions.
  4. Limitation: communication or power failure could prevent the robot completing its task.
  5. Judgement: despite the limitations, reducing human exposure may make the robot highly suitable.
35–42 min · Evaluation practice

6 · Build a balanced argument

Step 1: identify the task.
Step 2: identify the robot feature that helps.
Step 3: explain the benefit.
Step 4: identify a realistic limitation.
Step 5: explain the consequence.
42–47 min · Guided workout

7 · Scenario evaluation

A supermarket considers using mobile robots to move stock from a storage area to the shop floor. Explain why robots may be suitable and give one limitation.
Robots can repeatedly transport stock, reducing routine human workload. Sensors and programmed movement can help them navigate around obstacles. A limitation is that a fault or unexpected situation could stop delivery of stock, and purchase/maintenance costs may be high.
47–50 min · Exit ticket

8 · The final robotics question

Would a robot be suitable? Answer with: task requirement → robot feature → benefit → limitation → judgement.

🧠 Student Studio

Match · justify · evaluate · exam practice

🧭 Topic 6 roadmap

🎯 Learning targets

  • Explain why robots are suitable for particular tasks.
  • Use robot characteristics to justify a choice.
  • Identify realistic limitations.
  • Write balanced scenario-specific evaluations.
  • Reach a justified conclusion.

1 · Quick check

Which task is especially suited to a programmed robot?

2 · Workout — Match the feature

A robot is being considered for a dangerous environment. Which robot advantage should be used in the justification?
Safety: the robot can perform tasks in the hazardous environment while reducing the risk to a human operator.

3 · Interactive check

Why is a task with many unexpected situations potentially difficult for a conventional programmed robot?

4 · Worked Example — Warehouse robot

Task: transport boxes repeatedly between a storage area and a loading area.

Why suitable: the task is repetitive; the robot can follow programmed routes and use sensors to detect obstacles. Limitation: faults or unexpected obstacles may interrupt the task.

5 · Workout — Develop the answer

Develop this statement for warehouse robots: “They save time.”
A robot can repeatedly transport boxes without waiting for a human worker to perform every trip, so goods can be moved efficiently between locations.

6 · Interactive check

Which is a realistic limitation of a robot used in a factory?

7 · Scenario challenge — Exploration

A robot is used to inspect a dangerous area. Give one reason for using it and one limitation.
Reason: it can enter the dangerous area and collect information without directly exposing a person. Limitation: a power, communication or component failure could prevent the inspection.

8 · Exam-style question

Question: A company wants to use robots for repetitive manufacturing. Explain why robots are suitable and describe two possible disadvantages.

Robots are suitable because the task is repetitive and programmed movements can be repeated consistently and quickly. They may increase productivity and reduce human involvement in repetitive work. Disadvantages include the cost of purchasing and maintaining the robots, and faults or programming errors could stop production or cause incorrect actions.

9 · Final judgement challenge

A robot costs a lot to install but will perform a dangerous repetitive task for many years. Would the cost necessarily mean the robot is unsuitable?
No. Cost is only one factor. If the robot substantially reduces human exposure to danger and performs a repetitive task effectively for many years, the safety and productivity benefits may justify the cost.

10 · Exam writing frame

Robots are suitable because... the task is ________ and the robot can ________.

One disadvantage is... ________, which could result in ________.

Overall... the robot is/is not suitable because ________.

11 · Common mistakes

  • Assuming every task should be automated.
  • Giving generic advantages without linking them to the task.
  • Forgetting cost, maintenance and failure.
  • Ignoring unexpected situations.
  • Giving a conclusion without evidence from the scenario.

12 · Self-assessment

Can you justify whether a robot is suitable for a particular task?
