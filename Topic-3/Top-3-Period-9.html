Magnetic · optical · solid-state storage
Lesson overview

Period 9 · Storage Technologies

This lesson develops the third requirement of 3.3 Data storage: describing how magnetic, optical and solid-state (flash) storage operate and giving examples.

Success criteria: Students can explain the basic operation of each technology, use the required terminology, and give the correct examples.

Cambridge specifies: magnetic storage uses platters divided into tracks and sectors with electromagnets for reading/writing; optical storage uses lasers to create and read pits and lands; solid-state flash memory uses NAND or NOR technology with transistors acting as control and floating gates. Required examples include HDD, CD/DVD/Blu-ray, SSD, SD card and USB drive. citeturn0search12

My teaching route

Three technologies, three mechanisms

  1. Retrieve the three categories from Period 8.
  2. Teach magnetic storage: platters → tracks/sectors → electromagnets.
  3. Teach optical storage: laser → pits/lands → data.
  4. Teach flash storage: NAND/NOR → transistors → control/floating gates.

8 · Scenario challenge

A student says, “A DVD is solid-state because it stores data electronically.” Is this correct?

No. A DVD is optical storage. It uses a laser to read patterns of pits and lands.

A computer uses an HDD. State two terms that should appear in a description of how data is organised on the magnetic storage surface.

Tracks and sectors.

9 · Exam practice

Question: Describe the operation of magnetic storage.

Data is stored magnetically on platters. The platters are divided into tracks and sectors. Electromagnets are used to read and write the data.

Question: Describe the operation of optical storage.

A laser is used to create and read patterns called pits and lands on the optical disc.

Question: Describe the operation of solid-state flash memory.

Flash memory uses NAND or NOR technology. Transistors act as control gates and floating gates to store data.

10 · Common exam mistakes

  • Writing “HDD uses lasers” — it uses electromagnets.
  • Writing “DVD uses magnetic platters” — it uses optical pits and lands.
  • Forgetting tracks and sectors for magnetic storage.
  • Forgetting pits and lands for optical storage.
  • Giving “SSD” as an operation rather than explaining NAND/NOR and transistor gates.
